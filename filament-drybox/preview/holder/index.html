Frame and carriers

The frame has two legs per side, two upper spokes with R6 junction rounds, a central brace and three 45° rim undersides. Each frame plate has a 24.4 mm keyed slot down to a flat floor 13.2 mm below the axle; its thinned edges run in the carrier grooves. The carriers hold the 608s and lift out with the spindle; they seat by gravity only.

Spindle and collar

Two printed parts replace the cartridge. The spindle is one piece: both 608 journals (the drive journal keeps the hex key and clip groove the blue disc and clip were printed for), a Ø10 shaft, a threaded boss at the refill end (a 45° ramp joins its inboard face to the shaft, so that face needs no support when printed), and three 6 × 6 mm arms and struts carrying a 3.5 mm-long 45° cone ring at the drive end; the struts share the ring's inner radius, so each joins the full ring section. The collar screws onto the boss and carries the matching arms and cone ring. The cones seat in the spool bore's end chamfers, centering the spool and clamping its hub between them; that is the only spool contact. The clamp force closes through spindle, thread and collar, so the 608s carry no axial preload. Collar travel absorbs hub-width differences.

Thread: a custom printed thread, Ø18 major with 3 mm pitch, right-hand, made to fit only this spindle and collar (not a standard M18 nut). Friction is not relied on to hold the collar; the lock below does, in both turning directions, so the spool may feed and rewind either way.

Collar lock. Twelve slots are cut through the spindle's thread crests, 30° apart; the thread root stays whole and 47% of the crest length remains. A key on the collar rests in one slot, guided by a hole through the collar wall, and stops the collar turning either way once it has turned about ±4° (key play in the slot plus the guide hole; about ±0.034 mm of axial back-off). It cannot keep unscrewing. The key hangs from a 1 mm thick arm curved along the collar wall, in a pocket open through the collar's inboard end; the guide-hole roof stays closed, 0.75 mm above the key, so the hub stays whole; lifting the key 1.15 mm clears the crests. To lift it, push the paddle toward the arm's root (about 1 mm, roughly 4 N in a beam estimate): the curved arm swings the key outward, with about 1.1% strain at the arm's sharp root corner (estimate, untested). While turning the collar, hold the paddle there; let go and the key drops into the next slot. Lock positions are every 30°, or 0.25 mm of collar travel. To clamp, turn with the key lifted only until both cones just seat on the spool, then let go and keep turning forward until the key drops into the next slot. Never tighten firmly first, and do not back off to the previous slot. The forward index can add up to one lock step (0.25 mm), roughly 23–40 N of clamp depending on the assumed modulus and beam model; the actual value needs a physical trial. The paddle is reached from the refill side once the left carrier and 608 are off.

Strength screen (beam theory, assumed values, not a qualification). Per newton of axial clamp force: cone ring 0.22 MPa and 0.003 mm deflection, strut roots 0.23 MPa, arms 0.15–0.19 MPa, shaft 0.013 MPa. The lock key carries at most the collar cone's slip torque, about 0.93 N per newton of clamp force, on 2–3 crest ends (roughly 0.3–0.45 MPa per newton). With an assumed 10 MPa sustained stress for printed PLA and an assumed cone friction of 0.2, the ring, strut and arm screen alone is about 43 N (about 0.68 N·m). That is not an assembly limit: at the same assumed stress the lock key's crest bearing would govern at roughly 22–33 N, and lower if the key is partly raised under load. No clamp force or torque capacity is qualified. Tighten by hand only; over-tightening is not limited by design.

Axial float. The spindle, spool and collar can shift ±0.5 mm along the axle (0.2 mm thrust-sleeve gap, 0.1 mm 608 seat clearance, 0.2 mm carrier tongue play). Cones keep at least 0.5 mm to the frame plates at any float, or 0.25 mm with the collar at its 0.25 mm outboard limit. The reference spool's hub faces have 0.8 mm to the frame plates, 0.3 mm at full float. The reference spool's hub is 67.4 mm wide, slightly above the 67 mm design envelope.

Spool change. Lift the whole unit out of the frame. Pull the left carrier off, then slide the left 608 off its journal (a Ø7.9 printed slip fit; needs a physical trial). Hold the spool near the collar end, lift the key with the paddle and unscrew the collar about 3.8 turns from nominal (more if it was screwed further in), keeping the key lifted while turning. Slide the spool off toward the refill side. Reverse to fit a new spool, finishing at a lock position as above. The thrust sleeves still locate the spindle between both 608 inner races.

First-fit checks and remaining work

  1. Molded spool seat. The cones are sized to the printable spool's 45° bore chamfers. If a molded spool has a different chamfer or a sharp bore edge, the cones still center on that edge for bores from Ø49.2 to Ø56.2 (the cone's diameter range). The collar seats when twice the change in bore-plus-chamfer radius minus the change in hub width, relative to the reference, lies between −0.25 and +6 mm: hub widths 61.4–67.65 mm at the reference bore, a joint condition rather than independent ranges. Confirm on the first fit.
  2. Printed fits and the lock. 0.25 mm flank and radial thread clearances, the Ø7.9 left journal in the 608, the lock arm's spring and strain, key play and wear are untested.
