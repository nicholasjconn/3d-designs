The wall fitting and storage cap are now also on this plate. The fitting carries the wall O-ring groove and the cap carries the storage-seal interface; print these along with the inside nut for the complete wall assembly. Checks and limits

Loading saved evidence…

Claude independently checked the corrected crank, critical layers and matching meshes and found no remaining blockers for this first fit print. The local lip/flange calculation is a nominal hand-load screen, not a printed strength rating. The nut should be snug by fingers; it is not a wrench-tightened fastener.

Uprights are 74.8 mm apart: 3.5 mm per side against the measured 67.8 mm spool flange, with 3 mm after the modeled ±0.5 mm axial float. The nut is 4.2 mm long, its retaining plate 2.2 mm, and thread overlap is 3 mm at the assumed 2 mm wall.

The removable crank has aØ8.8 running shaft, giving0.8 mm radial clearance through most of theØ10.4 fitting bore. This gives alignment room without changing the overall length or the stationary sealing cap. Tighten the fitting/nut with open hand access, then lower the stand over the upward-open nut channel.

First physical checks: remove support; check the bearing pockets, journals and collar paddle; test the thread by fingers; test the empty lift-out assembly and crank withdrawal; then load the spool and turn it both ways. Confirm the actual bore/recess and wall thickness before drilling. The stationary carrier and nut faces have zero nominal gap; cleanup may be needed for a free slide. Printed strength, creep, wear and airtightness remain physical tests.
